Beginner Calisthenics Weekly Plan: A Complete Training Week

The best beginner plan is simple: full-body sessions 2 or 3 days a week, each with a push, a pull, legs and trunk. Total weekly work matters more than the number of days.

ProgramBeginnerFull body

In short

  • Train every major muscle group at least 2 days a week [1] [2].
  • Days matter less than you think: with equal total weekly work, splitting it over 2 or 4 days makes no clear difference to strength [3] or muscle growth [4].
  • Sets are what count: each extra weekly set was linked to more growth [5], and ACSM cites 10 or more weekly sets per muscle [2].
  • Start with 3 full-body sessions, 2–3 sets per movement, with no need to train to failure [2].
  • WATHBA rule: every two weeks add one set per movement or move to a harder version, not both.

Target muscles

Primary muscles

  • Push: chest, triceps and front shoulders [6]
  • Pull: back and biceps [7]
  • Legs: thighs and glutes [8]

Assisting and stabilising muscles

  • Trunk: abdominals and low back in the plank and every movement
  • Calves and grip

The plan covers the major muscle groups the NHS says to train: legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms [1].

How many days a week?

The minimum is 2 days for all major muscle groups [1]. A review of 22 studies found strength gains rise with sessions, but because total work rises; with volume equal, the number of days made no difference [3]. A review of 25 studies found the same for muscle growth [4].

The practical takeaway: pick the number of days you can stick to. For beginners we suggest 3 full-body sessions, which give enough volume with a rest day between sessions.

How many sets per muscle?

Muscle growth rises with weekly sets [5]; ACSM found 10 or more weekly sets per muscle enhance growth and 2–3 sets per exercise suffice for strength [2].

In our plan: 3 sessions × 3 sets = 9 weekly sets per main movement at first, reaching 10 and more as you add a set every two weeks.

How do you progress week by week?

The rule in each of our guides: once you hit the top of the range cleanly in two sessions in a row, move to the harder version. ACE progression chains show what the harder versions look like [9].

Bodyweight progression really builds strength: 3 days a week of progressive push-ups for 4 weeks raised strength like the bench press did [10].

These are the plan's stages over 12 weeks. Volume follows the sources [2] [5]; the timeline and the “Move on when” column are WATHBA working rules.

A starter week

Each session: a 5-minute warm-up, then the four movements in order: pull, push, legs, trunk. Put your hardest movement first, since exercises at the start of a session gain more strength [2].

Resting 60–120 seconds between sets is a WATHBA rule. Add at least 150 minutes of walking or other moderate aerobic activity a week [11].

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Training to failure every day. Three sessions are enough, and failure training does not reliably improve results [2].
  • Changing the program every week. Progress comes from adding sets and harder versions of the same movements [5].
  • Skipping legs and pulling. The plan covers every major muscle group on purpose [1].
  • Worrying about the number of days. Total weekly work matters more than how it is spread [3]; choose what you can stick to.

What do the sources say?

This is what each source behind this guide says, as stated in the source itself. Anything no source states, such as the move-on criteria and rest times, is a WATHBA working rule and is labelled as such.

  • NHS 2024 [1]: Strengthening activities for all major muscle groups (legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms) on at least 2 days a week, done to the point where you need a short rest before repeating.
  • ACSM 2026 [2]: An overview of 137 systematic reviews with more than 30,000 participants. Strength improved more with heavier loads, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets and at least 2 sessions a week. Hypertrophy improved with 10 or more weekly sets per muscle and eccentric overload. Training to momentary failure did not consistently change outcomes.
  • Sports Med 2018 [3]: A review of 22 studies: strength gains rose with weekly sessions, from 1 to 4 or more. But when total volume was equal, frequency had no clear effect; the benefit comes from total weekly work.
  • J Sports Sci 2019 [4]: A review of 25 studies: with weekly volume equal, how often a muscle is trained does not meaningfully change its growth, so pick the frequency that suits you.
  • J Sports Sci 2017 [5]: Each extra weekly set was linked to more muscle growth. Categories: under 5, 5–9 and 10 or more sets per muscle a week, with growth rising alongside volume.
  • ACE [6]: Targets arms, chest and shoulders. Brace the core, align the head with the spine, do not let the low back sag or the hips hike, and lower until the chest or chin touches the floor. Elbows close to the sides shift emphasis to the triceps and may reduce shoulder stress.
  • ACE [7]: Targets arms and back. Pull the shoulders back and down before pulling, avoid swinging, pull until the chin is level with the bar, then lower slowly until the arms are fully extended.
  • ACE [8]: A beginner exercise with no equipment. Feet slightly wider than hips; start by moving the hips back and down while the knees bend, back flat, and lower until the thighs are about parallel, or until the heels lift or the torso rounds. Knees track over the second toe; rise by pushing through the heels.
  • ACE 2018 [9]: Example progression chains to be matched to each person. Pull-up: hollow hold, straight-arm then flexed-arm hang, eccentric-only, partial, band-assisted, bodyweight, then weighted pull-ups. Push-up: plank, push-up-position plank, incline push-up with the incline continually lowered, band-assisted, eccentric-only, bottom-up, bodyweight, then weighted push-ups. Squat: assisted squat, bodyweight squat, then loaded squats.
  • J Strength Cond Res 2018 [10]: Progressive push-up variations, 3 days a week for 4 weeks, raised maximal bench-press strength much like bench-press training did. It was the first study to show that progressive calisthenics improves upper-body strength.
  • WHO 2020 [11]: All adults should do 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous aerobic activity a week, and regular muscle-strengthening activity is recommended for every age group.

Frequently asked questions

What is the best beginner calisthenics schedule?

Three full-body sessions a week on non-consecutive days, each with a pull, a push, legs and trunk, 2–3 sets per movement, gradually adding sets and difficulty.

Is 2, 3 or 4 days best?

Systematic reviews found the number of days matters little when total weekly work is equal. Two days is the minimum; three make it easier to reach enough volume.

How many sets per muscle?

ACSM cites 10 or more weekly sets per muscle for growth and 2–3 sets per exercise for strength. Start lower and build up.

Should I split body parts like in the gym?

Not necessary for beginners. Full-body sessions are simpler and train each muscle several times a week; total sets are what count in the end.

When will I see results?

In the progressive push-up study strength improved within 4 weeks, and in another study muscle size grew within 8 weeks. Results vary by person.
