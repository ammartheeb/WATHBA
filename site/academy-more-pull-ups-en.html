How to Do More Pull-ups: A Plan After Your First Rep

After your first pull-up, progress comes from total weekly work, not from maxing out every day. Spread your reps over clean sets and add sets gradually.

Pulling strengthIntermediatePull-up bar

In short

  • Weekly total is what counts: strength gains rose with sessions because volume rose; with volume equal, the number of days made no difference [1].
  • ACSM: 2–3 sets, a full range of motion and at least 2 sessions a week for strength, with eccentric emphasis for growth [2].
  • Do not go to failure every set; it did not reliably change results [2]. WATHBA rule: end each set one or two reps short of failure.
  • Slow negatives are harder and speed strength gains [3], a good way to add volume once your full reps run out.
  • Past 10 clean pull-ups, add weight; weighted pull-ups are the last step in the ACE ladder [4].

Target muscles

Primary muscles

  • The lats, the most active muscle in the pull-up [5]
  • The biceps, which work harder in the chin-up [5]

Assisting and stabilising muscles

  • Middle trapezius, which works harder with an overhand grip [6]
  • Forearms and brachioradialis, which work harder on the way up [6]

Changing grip shifts the work between muscles [5] [6], which is why the plan varies grips.

The secret is weekly volume

A review of 22 studies found people who train more often gain more strength, because they do more work; with volume equal, days made no difference [1]. Muscle growth rises with each extra weekly set [7].

In practice: if your max is 5, five sets of 3 clean reps beat one all-out set of 5. Spreading reps over sets below your max is a WATHBA working rule.

Slow negatives to add volume

A slow descent is harder and speeds strength gains, with likely soreness afterwards [3]. ACSM found eccentric emphasis enhances muscle growth [2].

Add 2–3 sets of slow negatives after your full sets, once you cannot do another clean full rep.

Vary your grip

In a chin-up, palms facing you, the biceps and chest work harder; in an overhand pull-up the lower trapezius works harder [5]. The overhand grip works the middle trapezius more than a neutral grip [6].

In the chin-up, draw the shoulders back and down, keep the wrists neutral, and never force the shoulder [8].

Progression ladder and when to move on

The volume stages, rep ranges and the “Move on when” column are WATHBA rules built on the principle of adding volume [1] [7]; added weight comes from the ACE chain [4].

A starter week

Two or three pull-up sessions a week, first in the session after the warm-up, since early exercises gain more strength [2]. Aim first for 10 or more weekly sets for the back [2].

Resting 2–3 minutes between pull-up sets is a WATHBA rule. Test your max only every 4 weeks, not every session.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Maxing out every day. Progress comes from total weekly work [1], not daily testing.
  • Half reps to pad the count. Start every rep from straight arms [9]; full range builds more strength [2].
  • Swinging and kicking. Pull without swinging [9]. Kipping reps do not count in this plan.
  • Failure on every set. Stop short of failure; failure training did not reliably improve results [2].

What do the sources say?

This is what each source behind this guide says, as stated in the source itself. Anything no source states, such as the move-on criteria and rest times, is a WATHBA working rule and is labelled as such.

  • Sports Med 2018 [1]: A review of 22 studies: strength gains rose with weekly sessions, from 1 to 4 or more. But when total volume was equal, frequency had no clear effect; the benefit comes from total weekly work.
  • ACSM 2026 [2]: An overview of 137 systematic reviews with more than 30,000 participants. Strength improved more with heavier loads, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets and at least 2 sessions a week. Hypertrophy improved with 10 or more weekly sets per muscle and eccentric overload. Training to momentary failure did not consistently change outcomes.
  • ACE 2015 [3]: Four preparatory exercises for the pull-up, including band-assisted pull-ups and eccentric chin-ups, at 2–4 sets of 8–12 repetitions. A slower lowering phase is harder and speeds strength gains, but soreness afterwards is more likely.
  • ACE 2018 [4]: Example progression chains to be matched to each person. Pull-up: hollow hold, straight-arm then flexed-arm hang, eccentric-only, partial, band-assisted, bodyweight, then weighted pull-ups. Push-up: plank, push-up-position plank, incline push-up with the incline continually lowered, band-assisted, eccentric-only, bottom-up, bodyweight, then weighted push-ups. Squat: assisted squat, bodyweight squat, then loaded squats.
  • J Strength Cond Res 2010 [5]: Measured muscle activity in 25 subjects. Highest was the latissimus dorsi, then biceps, infraspinatus, lower trapezius and chest. The chin-up activated the chest and biceps more; the pull-up activated the lower trapezius more.
  • J Electromyogr Kinesiol 2017 [6]: Compared four grips. The pronated grip activated the middle trapezius more than the neutral grip, and the pulling phase activated the biceps, brachioradialis and chest more than the lowering phase.
  • J Sports Sci 2017 [7]: Each extra weekly set was linked to more muscle growth. Categories: under 5, 5–9 and 10 or more sets per muscle a week, with growth rising alongside volume.
  • ACE [8]: A full grip with thumbs wrapped, palms facing you, abdominals braced, neutral wrists, shoulders back and down. Pull slowly without swinging until the chin is level with the bar, then lower slowly, without compromising the shoulder.
  • ACE [9]: Targets arms and back. Pull the shoulders back and down before pulling, avoid swinging, pull until the chin is level with the bar, then lower slowly until the arms are fully extended.

Frequently asked questions

How can I increase my pull-ups quickly?

Raise your total clean reps per week by spreading them over sets below your max, add slow negatives, and train 2–3 times a week.

Should I do pull-ups every day to get more?

Not necessary. A systematic review found the benefit comes from total work, not the number of days. Two or three sessions with enough volume do the job.

How many sets should I do?

Start with 4–6 short sets per session and build the weekly total to 10 or more, as ACSM cites for growth.

When should I add weight to pull-ups?

Once you pass 10 clean reps. Weighted pull-ups are the last step in the ACE progression ladder.

Do chin-ups help my pull-ups?

Yes. Both work the lats hard, and chin-ups add biceps work. Alternating adds volume without repeating the same load.
