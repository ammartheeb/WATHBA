How to Do Your First Pull-up: A Step-by-Step Ladder from Zero

A pull-up needs a strong back and arms, a steady grip and a braced trunk. If you cannot do one yet, trying it every day will not get you there; training its parts will: hangs, assisted pulls and slow negatives.

Pulling strengthBeginner to intermediatePull-up bar

In short

  • Follow the ACE ladder: straight-arm hang, flexed-arm hang, slow negatives, partial pull-ups, band-assisted pull-ups, then the full pull-up [1].
  • For preparatory drills such as band-assisted pull-ups and slow negatives, an ACE article suggests 2–4 sets of 8–12 reps [2].
  • Train at least 2 sessions a week; ACSM found this frequency improves strength [3].
  • The hardest-working muscle is the latissimus dorsi, then the biceps [4]. A chin-up grip with palms facing you uses the biceps and chest more [4].
  • WATHBA rule for moving on: complete each stage's dose in two sessions in a row before stepping up.

Target muscles

Primary muscles

  • Side of the back: latissimus dorsi, the most active muscle in the pull-up [4]
  • Front of the arm: biceps [4] [5]

Assisting and stabilising muscles

  • Back of the shoulder: infraspinatus [4]
  • Between the shoulder blades: lower and middle trapezius [4] [6]
  • Forearm: brachioradialis, which works more on the way up [6]
  • Trunk: chest, spinal erectors and obliques steady the body [4]

The pull-up starts at the shoulders, not the arms: the lower trapezius and chest begin the movement and the biceps and lats finish it [4]. That is why the ladder begins with scapular pulls.

What does a good pull-up look like?

Grip the bar palms forward and draw your shoulders back and down before bending your arms. Pull without swinging until your chin is level with the bar, then lower slowly to fully straight arms [5].

A full range, from straight arms to chin over the bar, is what builds strength; ACSM found strength improves more through a complete range of motion [3].

Pull-up or chin-up? How do grips differ?

In a chin-up your palms face you and the biceps and chest work harder. In a pull-up your palms face away and the lower trapezius works harder [4]. The lats work hard in both.

The overhand grip worked the middle trapezius more than a neutral grip with palms facing each other [6]. Pick the grip that suits your shoulders and vary it later.

Choosing a wall pull-up bar for home in Jordan

Before buying, measure the space above your head and in front of the bar, and check the wall type and fixing method. The bar's projection, such as 60 or 90 cm on the WATHBA bar, is the distance from bar to wall: the more projection, the further your body, face and knees stay from the wall as you move.

For slow negatives and band-assisted reps you need a safe way to reach the top of the bar, such as a sturdy step or box beneath it.

Progression ladder and when to move on

The ladder follows ACE's order of pull-up progressions [1]; doses come from ACE's preparatory-exercise article [2] or from WATHBA where no source gives a number. The “Move on when” column is a WATHBA working rule.

StageDoseHow to do itMove on when
Hollow hold3 × 15–20 secondsOn your back, lift shoulders and legs slightly and press your low back into the floor [1].You hold 3 × 20 seconds without the low back lifting.
Straight-arm hang3 × 15–30 secondsHang with shoulders drawn down, then practise pulling the shoulders down without bending the elbows [1] [5].You hang for 30 seconds three times, steadily.
Flexed-arm hang3 × 5–15 secondsUse a step to get your chin over the bar and hold there [1].You hold for 15 seconds three times.
Slow negatives3 × 3–5, 3–5-second loweringStart chin over the bar and lower slowly to straight arms. A slower descent is harder and speeds strength gains; expect some soreness afterwards [2].You control a 5-second descent on every rep.
Band-assisted pull-up2–4 × 8–12 [2]Loop a resistance band over the bar under your foot or knee. Move to lighter bands over time [1].You complete 3 × 8 with your lightest band.
Full pull-up3–5 attempts of 1–3 repsNo jumping or swinging, from straight arms to chin over the bar [5].You complete 3 × 5; then start adding weight [1].

A starter week

Train pull-ups 2 or 3 times a week with rest days between. ACSM found that 2 or more weekly sessions and 2–3 sets improve strength, and that exercises done at the start of a session gain more strength [3]. So put pull-up work first, right after the warm-up.

Work two neighbouring stages per session: your current stage and the one below for extra volume. Resting 90 seconds to 2 minutes between sets is a WATHBA rule.

DaySession
SundayWarm-up, 3 sets at your current stage, 2 at the easier stage, then hollow holds.
MondayNo pulling. Push-ups or legs are fine.
Tuesday3 sets at your current stage, trying to add one second or one rep.
ThursdaySlow negatives 3 × 3–5, then straight-arm hangs stopped short of full fatigue.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Trying a full pull-up every day. Repeating the same failed attempt daily does not build strength. Train the parts of the movement 2–3 times a week [3].
  • Swinging or kicking up to the bar. Pull under control without swinging [5], and go back to band-assisted reps if you need momentum.
  • Pulling with the arms while the shoulders creep up to the ears. Draw the shoulders back and down first [5]; that is what actually starts the movement [4].
  • Half reps from the top. Lower to fully straight arms on every rep [5] [3].
  • Dropping on the way down. The controlled descent is a core part of the exercise and speeds strength gains [2].

What do the sources say?

This is what each source behind this guide says, as stated in the source itself. Anything no source states, such as the move-on criteria and rest times, is a WATHBA working rule and is labelled as such.

  • ACE 2018 [1]: Example progression chains to be matched to each person. Pull-up: hollow hold, straight-arm then flexed-arm hang, eccentric-only, partial, band-assisted, bodyweight, then weighted pull-ups. Push-up: plank, push-up-position plank, incline push-up with the incline continually lowered, band-assisted, eccentric-only, bottom-up, bodyweight, then weighted push-ups. Squat: assisted squat, bodyweight squat, then loaded squats.
  • ACE 2015 [2]: Four preparatory exercises for the pull-up, including band-assisted pull-ups and eccentric chin-ups, at 2–4 sets of 8–12 repetitions. A slower lowering phase is harder and speeds strength gains, but soreness afterwards is more likely.
  • ACSM 2026 [3]: An overview of 137 systematic reviews with more than 30,000 participants. Strength improved more with heavier loads, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets and at least 2 sessions a week. Hypertrophy improved with 10 or more weekly sets per muscle and eccentric overload. Training to momentary failure did not consistently change outcomes.
  • J Strength Cond Res 2010 [4]: Measured muscle activity in 25 subjects. Highest was the latissimus dorsi, then biceps, infraspinatus, lower trapezius and chest. The chin-up activated the chest and biceps more; the pull-up activated the lower trapezius more.
  • ACE [5]: Targets arms and back. Pull the shoulders back and down before pulling, avoid swinging, pull until the chin is level with the bar, then lower slowly until the arms are fully extended.
  • J Electromyogr Kinesiol 2017 [6]: Compared four grips. The pronated grip activated the middle trapezius more than the neutral grip, and the pulling phase activated the biceps, brachioradialis and chest more than the lowering phase.

Frequently asked questions

How long until my first pull-up?

There is no fixed answer; it depends on your weight, current strength and consistency. We found no reliable source that gives a timeline, so we will not promise one. What matters is moving up the ladder week by week.

Is the pull-up harder than the chin-up?

Both work the lats hard. The chin-up uses more biceps and chest; the pull-up uses more lower trapezius. Start with the grip that feels best for your shoulders.

Should I train pull-ups every day?

It is not necessary. ACSM found that 2 or more sessions a week improve strength. We suggest 2–3 sessions with a rest day between them.

Do resistance bands help with pull-ups?

Yes. Band-assisted pull-ups are part of the ACE ladder toward a full pull-up and one of the preparatory drills an ACE article suggests at 2–4 sets of 8–12 reps.

Is a wall-mounted home bar suitable for a beginner?

Yes, as long as it is fixed to a suitable wall and you can reach it safely. Beginners use it for hangs, negatives and band-assisted reps before full pull-ups.

Is a 90 cm projection only for advanced athletes?

No. Projection is the distance from the bar to the wall and gives your body more room in front of the wall. It does not define your level.
