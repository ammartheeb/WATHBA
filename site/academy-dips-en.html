Parallel Bar Dips: From Support Holds to Your First Full Rep

In a dip you lower your body between two handles and press it back up. It is one of the strongest bodyweight exercises for the chest and triceps, and it starts with a steady support hold before any lowering.

Pushing strengthBeginner to intermediateParallel bars

In short

  • Bar dips target the chest and triceps effectively, and technique holds up well even with fatigue [1].
  • The order: bench dips, then bar dips, then ring dips. Muscle activity is higher on bars than on a bench, and rings are similar to bars with some muscles working harder [2].
  • If your shoulder is sensitive, be careful with bench dips: they need a greater shoulder-extension range [2].
  • Train at least 2 sessions a week with 2–3 sets [3].
  • WATHBA rule: do not start dips until you can hold 3 × 20 seconds in support with steady shoulders.

Target muscles

Primary muscles

  • Chest: pectoralis major [1]
  • Back of the arm: triceps [1] [2]

Assisting and stabilising muscles

  • Front shoulder and shoulder stabilisers, which work harder as you tire [1]
  • Trunk: holds your body position between the handles

In bench dips the triceps does most of the work; on bars and rings muscle activity rises further [2].

What does a good dip look like?

Start in support: arms straight, shoulders drawn down away from the ears, body still with no swinging. Lower by bending the elbows with a slight forward lean, then press until the arms are straight.

Depth: ACSM found a larger range builds more strength [3], but the dip takes the shoulder through a big range. WATHBA rule: lower only as deep as your shoulder stays comfortable with elbows close to your sides, and add depth gradually.

Bench, bar or ring dips?

Bench dips, with your hands on a bench behind you, focus on the triceps but push the shoulder into more extension; take care with them if your shoulder hurts [2].

Bar dips are the logical next step because muscle activity is higher [2]. Rings move, so they demand more control and make some muscles work harder; start them after mastering bars [2].

Which dip station suits your home?

A wall-mounted dip station saves floor space but needs a suitable wall and room for your knees. Floor parallel bars need level ground and room for the bases; the large version gives enough height to lower without your feet touching the floor. Measure your space before choosing.

Progression ladder and when to move on

The ladder starts with support, then moves from easier to harder dips as in the comparison of dip types [2]. Doses and the “Move on when” column are WATHBA working rules.

A starter week

Two or three sessions a week with a rest day between, and 2–3 sets at your stage [3]. Put dips first after the warm-up, since exercises at the start of a session gain more strength [3].

Resting 90 seconds to 2 minutes between sets is a WATHBA rule. Pair dips with pull-ups in the same session to balance pushing and pulling.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Shoulders shrugged up to the ears. Draw them down before every rep, and go back to support holds if you cannot.
  • Sinking deep at the shoulder's expense. Lower to a comfortable depth and add more gradually; the shoulder works through a big range in dips [2].
  • Starting on rings. Master bars first; rings need more control [2].
  • Swinging and kicking up. Use your feet as deliberate assistance on the floor or a step instead of momentum.

What do the sources say?

This is what each source behind this guide says, as stated in the source itself. Anything no source states, such as the move-on criteria and rest times, is a WATHBA working rule and is labelled as such.

  • Int J Environ Res Public Health 2022 [1]: In 15 men doing bar dips to exhaustion, technique did not change drastically with fatigue, while chest, triceps and three stabiliser muscles worked harder. The bar dip appears to target the chest and triceps effectively.
  • Int J Environ Res Public Health 2022 [2]: Compared bench, bar and ring dips. The bench dip mainly targets the triceps but needs a greater shoulder-extension range. The bar dip is an appropriate progression from the bench dip because muscle activity is higher, and the ring dip is similar to the bar dip with three muscles working harder. It flags the relevance for people with a history of shoulder pain or injury.
  • ACSM 2026 [3]: An overview of 137 systematic reviews with more than 30,000 participants. Strength improved more with heavier loads, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets and at least 2 sessions a week. Hypertrophy improved with 10 or more weekly sets per muscle and eccentric overload. Training to momentary failure did not consistently change outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

Which muscles do dips work?

Mainly the chest and triceps, plus the front shoulder, shoulder stabilisers and trunk. A study of bar dips found they target the chest and triceps effectively.

Which is easier, bench dips or bar dips?

Bench dips are lighter with less muscle activity, so bar dips are a suitable next step. But bench dips need more shoulder extension, so take care if your shoulder hurts.

How deep should I go in a dip?

To the depth where your shoulder stays comfortable and your elbows stay close, increasing it gradually. A larger range builds more strength, but not at the cost of pain.

Do I need floor bars or a wall-mounted station?

A wall station saves floor space but needs a suitable wall; floor bars need level ground and room for the bases. Choose by your space and wall type.

When should I move to ring dips?

Once you can do 3 clean sets of 10 bar dips. Rings give similar muscle activity to bars but demand more control.
