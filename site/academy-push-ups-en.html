Push-ups for Beginners: From Wall Press-ups to Your First Full Rep

The push-up builds the chest, triceps and shoulders and needs a braced trunk. If you cannot do a full rep yet, start at the wall and lower your body angle step by step until you reach the floor.

Pushing strengthBeginnerNo equipment needed

In short

  • Start at the stage where you can do 3 sets of 5–10 reps with a straight body. The NHS suggests the wall press-up at this dose for beginners [1].
  • Easiest to hardest: wall, raised surface, knees, full push-up, then feet raised. Force measurements confirm that knee and hands-raised push-ups are lighter and feet-raised push-ups heavier [2].
  • Train at least 2 days a week, as the NHS and ACSM recommend for strength work [3] [4].
  • WATHBA rule for moving on: once you complete 3 sets of 10 clean reps in two sessions in a row, go to the next stage.
  • If your wrists complain, press on handles or parallettes to keep the wrist straight; a study of wrist forces concluded that a neutral wrist is likely safer [5].

Target muscles

Primary muscles

  • Chest: pectoralis major [6]
  • Back of the arm: triceps [6]
  • Front of the shoulder: anterior deltoid [7]

Assisting and stabilising muscles

  • Side of the rib cage: serratus anterior, which steadies the shoulder blade [7]
  • Abdominals: rectus abdominis and external oblique, which stop the low back sagging [7] [6]

You do not need to isolate one muscle. The push-up works these muscles together; moving your hands only shifts the share of work between them.

What does a good push-up look like?

Brace your abdominals before you lower, and keep your head in line with your spine. Do not let the low back sag or the hips hike. Lower until your chest or chin touches the floor, then push the floor away from you [6].

Full range matters: the 2026 ACSM review found that strength improves more with a complete range of motion [4]. A full knee push-up beats a half push-up on the toes.

Where should my hands go?

Start with your hands roughly under your shoulders. When researchers compared narrow and wide hand positions, chest and triceps activity was higher with the narrow one [8].

Keeping the elbows close to your sides shifts work toward the triceps and may reduce stress on the shoulder joint [6]. If the front of your shoulder feels pinched, try this before adding reps.

Why is the push-up worth a place in your training?

The World Health Organization recommends regular muscle-strengthening activity at every age, alongside 150–300 minutes of moderate aerobic activity a week [9]. The push-up trains the chest, arms and shoulders with no equipment at all.

In a study that followed firefighters for 10 years, men who completed more than 40 push-ups had a markedly lower risk of heart disease than men completing fewer than 10 [10]. It is an association rather than proven cause, but it shows push-up count is a simple marker of fitness.

Nor is it a light exercise once you add resistance: banded push-ups produced muscle activity similar to a heavy bench press [7].

Progression ladder and when to move on

Each stage is harder because more of your body weight rests on your hands [2]. Doses are for beginners, and the “Move on when” column is a WATHBA working rule.

A starter week

Train push-ups on 2 or 3 days a week with a rest day between sessions. ACSM found that 2–3 sets and at least 2 sessions a week improve strength [4], and that training every set to failure did not consistently change results [4].

Resting 60–90 seconds between sets is a WATHBA rule. End a set when your form starts to break, not when you cannot move at all.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Low back sagging or hips hiking. Brace your abdominals and glutes before lowering, and drop a stage if your body will not stay in one line [6].
  • Half reps. Lower until your chest nears the floor; full range builds more strength [4]. Use an easier stage instead of shortening the movement.
  • Jumping to full push-ups too early. Stick to the move-on rule: 3 clean sets of 10 in two sessions in a row.
  • Wrist pain with the palm flat on the floor. Use handles or parallettes to keep the wrist straight [5], and stop if the pain persists.
  • Going to failure every day. Failure on every set does not reliably improve results [4], and rest between sessions is part of progress.

What do the sources say?

This is what each source behind this guide says, as stated in the source itself. Anything no source states, such as the move-on criteria and rest times, is a WATHBA working rule and is labelled as such.

  • NHS 2024 [1]: Wall press-up: stand at arm's length, hands flat at chest level, bend the arms slowly with elbows by your sides. Attempt 3 sets of 5–10 repetitions, at least twice a week, and build the repetitions up gradually.
  • J Strength Cond Res 2011 [2]: Measured ground reaction force in 6 push-up variations. Knee push-ups and hands-elevated push-ups were lighter than the regular push-up; feet-elevated push-ups were heavier. No difference was found between men and women.
  • NHS 2024 [3]: Strengthening activities for all major muscle groups (legs, hips, back, abdomen, chest, shoulders and arms) on at least 2 days a week, done to the point where you need a short rest before repeating.
  • ACSM 2026 [4]: An overview of 137 systematic reviews with more than 30,000 participants. Strength improved more with heavier loads, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets and at least 2 sessions a week. Hypertrophy improved with 10 or more weekly sets per muscle and eccentric overload. Training to momentary failure did not consistently change outcomes.
  • J Hand Ther 2018 [5]: Compared push-ups on a hyperextended wrist with a neutral wrist and concluded that the neutral wrist is likely safer, because ligaments may be preferentially loaded in hyperextension.
  • ACE [6]: Targets arms, chest and shoulders. Brace the core, align the head with the spine, do not let the low back sag or the hips hike, and lower until the chest or chin touches the floor. Elbows close to the sides shift emphasis to the triceps and may reduce shoulder stress.
  • Phys Sportsmed 2014 [7]: Elastic-resisted push-ups produced prime-mover activity similar to a heavy bench press while challenging the core more. Muscles measured included the chest, front deltoid, triceps, serratus anterior and abdominals.
  • J Strength Cond Res 2005 [8]: In 40 subjects, chest and triceps activity was greater with a narrow hand position than with a wide one.
  • WHO 2020 [9]: All adults should do 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous aerobic activity a week, and regular muscle-strengthening activity is recommended for every age group.
  • JAMA Netw Open 2019 [10]: Followed 1,104 male firefighters for 10 years. Those who completed more than 40 push-ups had a markedly lower risk of cardiovascular events than those completing fewer than 10. It is an association, not proof of cause, and the sample was men only.

Frequently asked questions

Should I do push-ups every day?

It is not necessary. The NHS recommends strength exercise on at least 2 days a week, and ACSM found 2 or more weekly sessions improve strength. For beginners we suggest 2–3 non-consecutive days.

How many push-ups should a beginner do?

The NHS suggests 3 sets of 5–10 wall press-ups. Start at the stage where you can complete that with a straight body, then add reps gradually.

Are wall push-ups actually useful?

Yes. It is the strength exercise the NHS suggests for beginners and the lightest push-up form, so it builds a base before incline and floor push-ups.

Do push-ups build the chest?

The push-up mainly works the chest. ACSM's 2026 review found muscle growth improves with 10 or more weekly sets per muscle, and banded push-ups approach the muscle activity of a heavy bench press.

Are push-ups suitable for women?

Yes, with the same progression. NHS and WHO guidance covers all adults, and the study measuring push-up forces found no difference between men and women across the variations.

What is the difference between floor push-ups and parallettes?

On parallettes the wrist stays straight instead of bending back, which a wrist-force study concluded is likely safer. The height also lets you go deeper once you are ready.
