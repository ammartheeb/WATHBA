Jump Rope for Beginners: Technique, a Starter Plan and Calories

Jumping rope is vigorous cardio in a small space. You start with a low jump, a soft landing and short rounds, then lengthen the rounds over time.

ConditioningBeginnerJump rope

In short

  • Jumping rope is vigorous: 8.3 METs at a slow pace, 11.8 moderate and 12.3 fast, over 8 times resting energy use [1].
  • WHO recommends 75–150 minutes a week of vigorous or 150–300 of moderate activity [2]. Jumping rope counts as vigorous.
  • The ACE beginner plan: 18 rounds of 15 seconds work and 15 seconds rest, with easy moves such as jogging in place [3].
  • If you are new to high-impact exercise, start with the beginner moves, warm up and cool down, and check with a doctor first [3].
  • WATHBA rule: increase one thing a week, duration, rounds or speed, never all three.

Target muscles

Primary muscles

  • Calves: they absorb every jump and landing on the balls of the feet
  • Heart and lungs: rope jumping is vigorous aerobic work [1]

Assisting and stabilising muscles

  • Thighs and glutes in the soft landing
  • Forearms and shoulders to turn the rope, and the abdominals for a steady trunk

The muscle description here is a WATHBA anatomical note, since the cited sources measure intensity, not muscles.

Technique: quiet hands, small jump

Stand tall with elbows close to your body and relaxed shoulders. Turn the rope from the wrists, not the shoulders. Jump only high enough for the rope to pass, and land on the balls of your feet with soft knees.

If the rope keeps catching, practise the rhythm without a rope first, or hold both handles in one hand and turn it at your side as you jump.

How many calories does jumping rope burn?

Intensity is measured in METs, how many times your energy use exceeds rest. Slow rope jumping is 8.3 METs, moderate 11.8 and fast 12.3 [1].

A rough estimate using the common formula, calories ≈ MET × body weight in kg × hours: a 70 kg person jumping slowly for 10 actual minutes burns roughly 95 calories. This is a WATHBA estimate; it varies by person, and rest between rounds does not count.

How to combine it with calisthenics

If pull-up or push-up strength is your goal, put the rope after strength work or on a separate day, so you are not tired before the main work. Two easy minutes make a good warm-up. The rope helps you reach your weekly aerobic minutes [2].

Choose a level, non-slip floor and suitable shoes, and make sure the ceiling and surroundings are clear. If jumping hurts a joint, stop.

Progression ladder and when to move on

Levels and work-rest times come from the ACE workout [3]. The no-rope stage and the “Move on when” column are WATHBA working rules.

A starter week

2–3 sessions a week are enough to start. The ACE session takes 15–25 minutes including rest, with a warm-up and cool-down [3].

Watch your calves the next day. If they are still tired or sore, cut rounds before adding more. WATHBA rule: do not jump daily in the first weeks.

DaySession

Thursday or FridayA second ACE session, or a brisk walk if your calves are tired.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Jumping far too high. Jump only high enough for the rope to pass.
  • Turning the rope from the shoulders. Elbows close, the movement from the wrists.
  • Landing hard on the heels. Land on the balls of your feet with soft knees.
  • Raising duration, speed and rounds together. Change one thing a week.

What do the sources say?

This is what each source behind this guide says, as stated in the source itself. Anything no source states, such as the move-on criteria and rest times, is a WATHBA working rule and is labelled as such.

  • Compendium of Physical Activities 2024 [1]: Intensity in METs (multiples of resting energy use): rope jumping slow, under 100 skips a minute, 8.3; moderate, 100–120 skips, 11.8; fast, 120–160 skips, 12.3; double unders, 10.0.
  • WHO 2020 [2]: All adults should do 150–300 minutes of moderate or 75–150 minutes of vigorous aerobic activity a week, and regular muscle-strengthening activity is recommended for every age group.
  • ACE 2015 [3]: A circuit of 18 sets with equal work and rest: 15 seconds for beginners, 30 for intermediate and 60 for advanced, 15–25 minutes in total including rest. Beginner moves: forward and backward jogging and hop-scotch jumps. Anyone new to high-impact work starts with the beginner moves, warms up and cools down, and checks with a physician first.

Frequently asked questions

How many calories does jumping rope burn?

Slow rope jumping is 8.3 METs per the Compendium of Physical Activities. As an estimate, a 70 kg person burns roughly 95 calories in 10 actual minutes of slow jumping, more at higher speed. It varies by person.

How many minutes a day should I jump rope?

No need for daily sessions. Start with the ACE beginner session, 18 rounds of 15 seconds, 2–3 times a week, and build up gradually.

Is jumping rope a complete workout?

It is excellent aerobic work, but WHO also recommends muscle-strengthening exercise. Combine it with calisthenics.

Why does the rope keep catching my feet?

Usually timing, or turning the rope from the shoulders. Practise the rhythm without a rope, keep your elbows close, and check the rope length suits you.

Is jumping rope suitable for everyone?

It is high-impact. If you are new to that, start with the beginner moves, check with a doctor first, and stop if your joints hurt.
