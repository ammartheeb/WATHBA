How to Train on Monkey Bars: Grip and Traversing Step by Step

Monkey bars are a row of rungs you cross hand over hand. The challenge is not grip alone: you shift your weight from hand to hand with calm timing, and you plan how to get down before you tire.

Pulling and gripBeginner to intermediateMonkey bars

In short

  • Monkey bars combine hanging and pulling: the lats and biceps do the most work when pulling [1], and the forearms carry your weight the whole way.
  • Hang before you traverse: straight-arm then flexed-arm hangs [2], shoulders down and no swinging [3].
  • WATHBA rule: do not start traversing until you can hang 30 seconds three times, and always plan a mid-route exit.
  • A strong grip matters for health too: weaker grip was linked to higher mortality in a Lancet study [4].
  • Train at least twice a week [5], minding forearm fatigue if you also do pull-up sessions that week.

Target muscles

Primary muscles

  • Forearms and grip: they carry your weight at every moment, with the brachioradialis working harder when you pull [6]
  • Back: the lats, the most active muscle in pulling movements [1]

Assisting and stabilising muscles

  • Biceps and the back of the shoulder [1]
  • Trunk: stops excess swinging between reaches

The cited studies measured pull-ups, not monkey bars; we use them because traversing relies on the same hanging and pulling.

Plan your start and exit before you hang

Check the rungs are dry, the spacing suits you and the ground below is safe to drop onto. Decide how you reach the first rung and how you get down mid-route if you tire. WATHBA rule: letting go at failure is not an exit plan.

Learn the hand transfer before lifting your feet

On a low setup, keep your feet on the ground and hold a rung with both hands. Shift some weight to one hand, then free the other and place it on the next rung without jerking your body. Once that is easy, reduce foot support gradually.

While hanging, keep your shoulders drawn down and avoid swinging [3]. A small, calm sway helps timing; kicking and lunging for far rungs raises the risk of slipping.

Grip strength or grip endurance?

Monkey bars need grip endurance more than maximal grip, because your hands hold on the whole route. Progress by adding reaches or hang time, not both at once. Details are in the grip guide.

A starter week

At least two sessions a week [5] with a rest day between. If you also do pull-up sessions, put monkey bars on a different day or after pull-ups at lower volume, since both tire the forearms.

Resting 90 seconds between rounds is a WATHBA rule.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Kicking and lunging for far rungs. Shift your weight calmly and avoid swinging [3].
  • Starting with a full route. Start with supported reaches, then a short route.
  • No exit plan. Decide before you start how to get down mid-route.
  • Monkey bars right after a heavy pull-up session. Your forearms are tired and your grip slips sooner. Cut the volume or change the day.

What do the sources say?

This is what each source behind this guide says, as stated in the source itself. Anything no source states, such as the move-on criteria and rest times, is a WATHBA working rule and is labelled as such.

  • J Strength Cond Res 2010 [1]: Measured muscle activity in 25 subjects. Highest was the latissimus dorsi, then biceps, infraspinatus, lower trapezius and chest. The chin-up activated the chest and biceps more; the pull-up activated the lower trapezius more.
  • ACE 2018 [2]: Example progression chains to be matched to each person. Pull-up: hollow hold, straight-arm then flexed-arm hang, eccentric-only, partial, band-assisted, bodyweight, then weighted pull-ups. Push-up: plank, push-up-position plank, incline push-up with the incline continually lowered, band-assisted, eccentric-only, bottom-up, bodyweight, then weighted push-ups. Squat: assisted squat, bodyweight squat, then loaded squats.
  • ACE [3]: Targets arms and back. Pull the shoulders back and down before pulling, avoid swinging, pull until the chin is level with the bar, then lower slowly until the arms are fully extended.
  • The Lancet 2015 [4]: Nearly 140,000 people in 17 countries: each 5 kg lower grip strength was linked to a 16% higher risk of death from any cause and 17% higher cardiovascular death, and grip was a stronger predictor than systolic blood pressure. It is an association; the authors call for research on whether improving strength lowers the risk.
  • ACSM 2026 [5]: An overview of 137 systematic reviews with more than 30,000 participants. Strength improved more with heavier loads, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets and at least 2 sessions a week. Hypertrophy improved with 10 or more weekly sets per muscle and eccentric overload. Training to momentary failure did not consistently change outcomes.
  • J Electromyogr Kinesiol 2017 [6]: Compared four grips. The pronated grip activated the middle trapezius more than the neutral grip, and the pulling phase activated the biceps, brachioradialis and chest more than the lowering phase.

Frequently asked questions

Is monkey bars a back or a grip exercise?

Both. Forearms and grip carry your weight the whole way, while the back and biceps work as you pull and reach for the next rung.

Hand over hand, or both hands on each rung?

Start with both hands on each rung because it is easier, then go hand over hand once your reaches are calm.

Do monkey bars replace a pull-up bar?

Not fully. Monkey bars add travel and endurance; a pull-up bar builds pulling strength in one place. They complement each other.

Should I wear gloves?

Not necessary. Dry rungs and hands matter most. If your hands sweat a lot, grip tape on the rungs is an option.
