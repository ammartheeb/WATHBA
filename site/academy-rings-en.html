Gymnastic Rings for Beginners: Setup, Support, Pulling and Pushing

Rings move in every direction, so your muscles must work to steady them. That is why a simple push-up or dip gets harder on rings. Start with movements where your feet stay on the floor.

Control and stabilityIntermediateGymnastic rings

In short

  • Ring instability raises muscle demand: in the muscle-up the arms and forearms worked harder on rings than on a bar [1], and in dips three muscles worked harder [2].
  • Suspended push-ups, as on rings, are highly effective for the abdominals, while stable push-ups give the chest and front shoulder more work [3].
  • Learn a movement on a bar or parallel bars first, then take it to rings; researchers suggest learning the muscle-up on a bar first because it is easier [1].
  • Train at least 2 sessions a week [4].
  • WATHBA rule: do not move to feet-off-the-floor movements until you can hold 3 × 20 seconds in a ring support without heavy shaking.

Target muscles

Primary muscles

  • Pulling on rings: back, biceps and forearms, with arms and forearms working harder because of the instability [1]
  • Pushing on rings: chest and triceps [2] [3]

Assisting and stabilising muscles

  • Abdominals: suspended push-ups work them hard [3]
  • Upper trapezius and forearms to control the rings [1]

Rings do not add new muscles; they make the same muscles work harder to keep the rings still.

How do you hang rings safely?

Hang both straps from a solid point that holds your weight in motion, such as a properly fixed pull-up bar, check the rings are level and the buckles are locked before loading them. WATHBA rule: test the setup with your feet on the floor before any movement.

Change difficulty with ring height: low rings for push-ups and rows, high rings for support and dips.

Why are rings harder than a bar?

A bar stays put; rings move. Comparing ring and bar muscle-ups, the arms, forearms and upper trapezius worked harder on rings, with little change in shoulder stabilisers [1]. In dips, muscle activity on rings was similar to bars, with three muscles working harder [2].

Ring push-ups shift the work: more for the abdominals and triceps, while the chest and front shoulder work harder in stable push-ups [3].

Progression ladder and when to move on

The ladder runs from most to least stable, based on the bar-versus-ring comparisons [2] [1]. Doses and the “Move on when” column are WATHBA working rules.

StageDoseHow to do itMove on when

Ring push-up3 × 5–10Start with the rings a little above the floor and keep them close to your body [3].You complete 3 × 10 without the rings drifting.

Ring pull-up3 × 3–6After a full bar pull-up; let the rings rotate naturally with your hands.You complete 3 × 6; then learn the muscle-up on a bar first [1].

A starter week

At least two sessions a week [4]. Start each session with a pull, a push and a support hold, 2–3 sets each [4].

Resting 90 seconds between sets is a WATHBA rule. When the rings start shaking hard or drifting away, the set is over.

DaySession

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Rings drifting away from the body. Keep them against your body, and drop to a more stable movement if you cannot.
  • Starting with fully hanging movements. Start with rows and push-ups with your feet down, then support holds.
  • Learning a skill straight on rings. Learn it on a bar or parallel bars first; rings are harder [1].
  • Rings at different heights. Set them level and check the buckles before every session.

What do the sources say?

This is what each source behind this guide says, as stated in the source itself. Anything no source states, such as the move-on criteria and rest times, is a WATHBA working rule and is labelled as such.

  • Int J Exerc Sci 2023 [1]: The ring muscle-up worked the arms, forearms and upper trapezius more than the bar version because of the instability, without much change in shoulder stabilisers. The authors suggest learning the bar muscle-up first because it is less difficult.
  • Int J Environ Res Public Health 2022 [2]: Compared bench, bar and ring dips. The bench dip mainly targets the triceps but needs a greater shoulder-extension range. The bar dip is an appropriate progression from the bench dip because muscle activity is higher, and the ring dip is similar to the bar dip with three muscles working harder. It flags the relevance for people with a history of shoulder pain or injury.
  • Phys Sportsmed 2014 [3]: Elastic-resisted push-ups produced prime-mover activity similar to a heavy bench press while challenging the core more. Muscles measured included the chest, front deltoid, triceps, serratus anterior and abdominals.
  • ACSM 2026 [4]: An overview of 137 systematic reviews with more than 30,000 participants. Strength improved more with heavier loads, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets and at least 2 sessions a week. Hypertrophy improved with 10 or more weekly sets per muscle and eccentric overload. Training to momentary failure did not consistently change outcomes.

Frequently asked questions

Are gymnastic rings suitable for beginners?

Yes, with feet-down movements such as rows and push-ups. Fully hanging moves such as dips and pull-ups come after mastering them on a bar and parallel bars.

Why are ring push-ups harder?

Because the rings move, your abdominals and triceps work harder to steady you. A study found suspended push-ups highly effective for the abdominals.

Where should I hang rings at home?

From a solid point that holds your moving body weight, such as a properly fixed pull-up bar. Never hang them from a door, pipes or furniture.

Should I learn the muscle-up on rings or a bar?

On a bar first. The study comparing the two suggested the bar muscle-up is easier to learn, because rings demand more from the arms.
