Grip Strength for Calisthenics: Hangs, Hand Grippers and Grip Tape

Grip is not one ability: closing a hand gripper is one thing, holding a bar while you hang is another. Train the kind that limits your training.

Grip and forearmsAll levelsHanging

In short

  • Grip strength is a health marker: in a study of nearly 140,000 people in 17 countries, each 5 kg lower grip was linked to a 16% higher risk of death from any cause [1].
  • For pull-ups and monkey bars, the key drill is hanging itself: start with a straight-arm hang, then a flexed-arm hang [2].
  • The forearm works harder on the way up in a pull-up [3], which is why grip often tires first in beginners.
  • Train grip 2 or 3 days a week inside your sessions, not daily [4].
  • Grip tape improves the handle surface but does not strengthen the hand. WATHBA rule: never use it to hide a crack or a loose handle.

Target muscles

Primary muscles

  • Forearm: the finger flexors, and the brachioradialis, which works harder on the way up [3]
  • The muscles of the hand itself

Assisting and stabilising muscles

  • The biceps when the arms bend in a hang [5]
  • Shoulders and back to hold the hanging position [6]

The hand and finger-flexor description is a WATHBA anatomical note; the cited studies measured only the brachioradialis and biceps.

Three kinds of grip

Crushing strength: closing your hand against resistance, as with a hand gripper. Hanging endurance: holding a bar for a long time under your body weight. Surface: if the handle is wet or slick, your hand slips even when it is strong.

WATHBA rule: do not expect a gripper to raise your hang time automatically. If your goal is pull-ups or monkey bars, make hanging your main drill and the gripper an extra.

Why does grip strength matter?

In the PURE study in The Lancet, grip strength predicted cardiovascular death better than systolic blood pressure: each 5 kg lower was linked to 17% higher cardiovascular death [1]. It is an association, and the authors themselves call for research on whether building grip lowers the risk [1].

When does grip tape help?

Grip tape changes the handle surface to reduce slipping, especially with sweat. It does not strengthen the hand or treat pain. Fit it to a clean, dry, sound handle and replace it when the edges lift.

Progression ladder and when to move on

The hanging stages follow the ACE pull-up chain [2]. Doses, the “Move on when” column and the gripper stages are WATHBA working rules.

A starter week

Add grip work at the end of 2 or 3 sessions a week, after pull-ups rather than before, so your hands are fresh for the main work. 2–3 sets are enough [4].

Resting 60–90 seconds is a WATHBA rule. Lasting forearm pain is not a sign of success; cut the volume if it is still there the next day.

Common mistakes and fixes

  • Holding with the fingertips. Wrap your fingers and thumb fully around the bar.
  • Training grip before pull-ups. Do it after, so tired hands do not limit your main work.
  • Relying only on the gripper. If hanging is the goal, train hanging itself [2].
  • Taping over a damaged handle. Check the handle first; tape does not fix a fault in the equipment.

What do the sources say?

This is what each source behind this guide says, as stated in the source itself. Anything no source states, such as the move-on criteria and rest times, is a WATHBA working rule and is labelled as such.

  • The Lancet 2015 [1]: Nearly 140,000 people in 17 countries: each 5 kg lower grip strength was linked to a 16% higher risk of death from any cause and 17% higher cardiovascular death, and grip was a stronger predictor than systolic blood pressure. It is an association; the authors call for research on whether improving strength lowers the risk.
  • ACE 2018 [2]: Example progression chains to be matched to each person. Pull-up: hollow hold, straight-arm then flexed-arm hang, eccentric-only, partial, band-assisted, bodyweight, then weighted pull-ups. Push-up: plank, push-up-position plank, incline push-up with the incline continually lowered, band-assisted, eccentric-only, bottom-up, bodyweight, then weighted push-ups. Squat: assisted squat, bodyweight squat, then loaded squats.
  • J Electromyogr Kinesiol 2017 [3]: Compared four grips. The pronated grip activated the middle trapezius more than the neutral grip, and the pulling phase activated the biceps, brachioradialis and chest more than the lowering phase.
  • ACSM 2026 [4]: An overview of 137 systematic reviews with more than 30,000 participants. Strength improved more with heavier loads, a full range of motion, 2–3 sets and at least 2 sessions a week. Hypertrophy improved with 10 or more weekly sets per muscle and eccentric overload. Training to momentary failure did not consistently change outcomes.
  • J Strength Cond Res 2010 [5]: Measured muscle activity in 25 subjects. Highest was the latissimus dorsi, then biceps, infraspinatus, lower trapezius and chest. The chin-up activated the chest and biceps more; the pull-up activated the lower trapezius more.
  • ACE [6]: Targets arms and back. Pull the shoulders back and down before pulling, avoid swinging, pull until the chin is level with the bar, then lower slowly until the arms are fully extended.

Frequently asked questions

Is a hand gripper enough for pull-ups?

No. A gripper builds hand closing, while pull-ups need hanging endurance under your body weight. Make hanging your main drill and the gripper an extra.

Should I train grip every day?

No need. Two or three days a week inside your sessions is enough, with rest days for your hands.

Why is grip strength said to matter for health?

Because a large Lancet study found weaker grip linked to higher risk of death and heart disease, a stronger predictor than systolic blood pressure. It is an association, not proof that grip training alone protects you.

Does forearm pain mean the training is working?

No. Mild fatigue is normal; pain that lasts into the next day means too much volume. Cut back.

Does grip tape make my grip stronger?

No. It only improves the handle surface and reduces slipping.
